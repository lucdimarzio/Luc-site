To understand worship as a spiritual discipline, you first have to understand that music is just one expression of worship. We tend to connect the songs we sing on Sunday with worship, and we rarely stop to think about what worship actually is. We're also capable of worshiping a lot of things that aren't God, so the object of our worship, Jesus Christ, matters.

True Christian worship is our response to the love we experience from the heart of God the Father. People encounter God in a lot of ways, in a church service or out in nature, in stillness or in a crowd. Our response to that encounter is worship.

> In view of God's mercy, offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God. This is true and proper worship. (Romans 12:1)

### 1. Understand worship as more than music

Worship is offering ourselves fully to God, all of who we are, laid before Him.

**Focus on the object of worship.** We worship God because of who He is, not for what we might get from Him. When we sing, we can start expecting God to return the favor and bless us. He can, but He isn't obligated to. The presence of God isn't a feeling. Our worship should keep the same energy whether He's pouring out blessing or seems quiet for a season. He's still the same God.

**Offer a sacrifice of worship.** There will be times you don't feel like worshiping. That's okay. It's one more reason our faith isn't built on emotion. In those moments, pray something like, "Lord, I don't feel like worshiping, but I want to give You this time. It belongs to You." And worship anyway.

### 2. Cultivate a lifestyle of worship

When the music ends on Sunday, our worship doesn't. It isn't limited to worship nights. Listen for God's voice throughout the day, not just at church or during your quiet time. The more you listen for His voice, the more you'll hear it.

**Practice the presence of God.** Whisper praise and thanksgiving all day long. The ordinary tasks stop being burdens and become chances to worship. Take any area of your life, like marriage, parenting, work, serving, rest, and ask how God's greatness has shaped it. Then keep asking. That's a lifetime habit.

### 3. Make worship a community discipline

There will be plenty of times for private worship and personal revelation, but worship works itself out best in community. Gathering isn't a time to separate yourself from everyone else. It's a chance for the whole local body to cry out to God together. Everyone stands on level ground before Him.

**Prepare for worship.** We rush into church at the last minute, or rush into prayer. Take five minutes beforehand and be still. Not to chase a feeling, but so you're ready to hear what He's saying.

**Worship in spirit and truth.** Jesus told the woman at the well that true worshipers worship in spirit and truth (John 4:23-24). It's not a place, a ritual or a tradition. It comes from within, moved by the Spirit and grounded in the truth of who God is.

**Worship with your whole self.** The Bible describes worship in physical terms. The Hebrew word for worship means to bow down, and Scripture is full of kneeling, lifting hands, clapping, standing and bowing. Worship isn't about what other people think of you. If everyone in the room is bringing themselves before God, nobody is worried about how you look.

### Practice. Prepare. Participate.

Worship is a discipline because we're easily tempted to neglect it, or to go through the motions on autopilot. So practice the presence of God every day, prepare your heart before you gather, and participate with your whole self.

At the core of every spiritual discipline is this: God is after our heart, and He'll take care of our abilities.

> Come, let us worship and bow down, let us kneel before the Lord our Maker. (Psalm 95:6)
