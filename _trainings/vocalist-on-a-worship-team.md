Your body is your instrument. It's the only instrument not made by human hands, and it's uniquely made to reflect God's glory. The goal is to use it to worship God and to lead your team well.

### Know and take care of your voice

- **Sleep.** Rest gets you ready vocally and mentally.
- **Hydrate.** Keep drinking water so your vocal cords don't strain.
- **Warm up.** Use good technique before you sing.
- **Know your range.** Know your lowest and highest notes, and speak up if a song you're leading sits outside them.
- **Find your break.** Know where you shift from chest voice to head voice.

### Three things to focus on

- **Technical.** Melody, harmony, intonation, diction and blend.
- **Physical.** Breathing, stance, posture, stage presence, managing nerves, and a worshipful posture while you lead.
- **Spiritual.** Stay connected to God through His Word, respect leadership, trust your team, and work toward the same goal together.

> Be in the Word. This will give you a deeper connection to the Lord and the songs you are singing to Him and about Him.

### The technical side

- **Melody.** Learn the main part first so you have a strong foundation.
- **Harmony.** Practice finding parts that complement the melody.
- **Intonation.** Know where you tend to go flat or sharp.
- **Diction.** Enunciate clearly and consistently with the rest of the team. Pronunciation isn't the same as enunciation.

### Blending: singing as one voice

Blending means matching four things so the team sounds like one voice:

- **Timbre.** Adjust your tone and brightness. Overly bright or heavy voices stick out.
- **Style.** Use the same approach to vibrato, runs and articulation.
- **Tuning.** Listen closely and fix flat or sharp spots.
- **Breath.** Plan breaths together for unified phrasing and better stamina.

### Preparation and practice

Get rest the night before. Learn the melody, harmonies and lyrics. Drink water all day. Warm up with things like lip trills, tongue twisters and arpeggios. Rehearse with the team so you're blending and phrasing together. And listen to feedback from your worship leader and teammates, then make adjustments.
