I see the worship leader as the spiritual authority in the band, the person who sets the tone for both skill and character and lives both out at a high level for the rest of the team.

### Holistic growth

Five areas we all keep growing in, and that I want our leaders to value and model:

- Spiritual health
- Emotional health
- Leadership
- Technical skill
- Physical health

### The fruit of a healthy leader

When those areas are healthy, the fruit shows up. Some of it comes naturally to a leader and some of it doesn't:

- Hospitality
- Teaching
- Equipping
- A desire to empower others
- Making disciples
- Diligent in skill, humble in heart

### Three responsibilities

1. **Equip the band and singers.** Everybody gets the same information and the resources they need to do their best.
2. **Mobilize and stir the hearts of the congregation.** Leaders are empowered to lead out of their own strengths.
3. **Serve as a spiritual presence and authority.** That usually starts in the conversations we have around communion and the books we study together. It's on the leader to prepare their heart and mind for those conversations and to look for chances to lead spiritually.

Our job as staff is to give worship leaders the tools to do all of this, with open communication and the freedom to lead in the ways they choose.

### What it's all working toward

1. That people would go into their everyday lives talking about and living out what happened in the gathering on Sunday.
2. That people would feel like they encountered God, and leave wanting a deeper relationship with Jesus.
3. That people would feel empowered to take personal responsibility for their faith.

We're not living for Sunday anymore. Sunday is great, but it's not the thing. We're called to make disciples, not to get people to sing more intensely, and discipleship happens in life, not on a stage in a room full of people we don't know. If singing songs changed lives, they would have done so by now.
