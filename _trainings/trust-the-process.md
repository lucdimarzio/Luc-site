The most important thing we do is point people to Jesus. The church mostly has two tools for that: music and speech. But not everyone who walks in on a Sunday connects with music, and not everyone connects with hearing someone speak.

A creative team exists because there's a potential third way to point people to Jesus. So each week we work together to:

1. Enhance what we're already doing
2. Find a third way to point people to Jesus
3. Help and encourage other ministries to do the same

### Made in His image

Creativity isn't just for artists. It's inside all of us. Remember being a kid? Finger painting, Legos, doodling, imaginary friends. We were all creative once, and we still are.

### The six I's of the creative process

1. **Inform.** Gather information and identify the problem to solve or the place to begin. Seek inspiration from Scripture, current events, your own experiences, and the world around you. Ask questions that challenge assumptions.
2. **Inspire.** Be curious. Throw spaghetti at the wall. Brainstorm without judgment and say "yes, and" more than "yeah, but." Sometimes the worst ideas lead to the best ones.
3. **Ideate.** Take the best ideas and shape them to work in our context.
4. **Integrate.** Bring order to the chaos. Line up schedules, people and resources, and bring structure to the idea. If several teams are involved, someone owns getting it across the finish line.
5. **Improve.** Sharpen every detail. Ask for opinions and feedback.
6. **Implement.** Do the thing. Even once it's out in the world, there may still be room to refine it.

The first few steps can't only happen in a meeting. Keep your radar up when you watch something, go to a show or a concert, and take notes on how other environments use creativity.

### Planning ahead

We plan on a rolling six-week window that lines up with the process. Things six weeks out are in inform and brainstorm mode. Things one or two weeks out are in improve and implement mode. We rarely go back to brainstorming in the last two weeks unless something truly breaks, and late changes should meet some healthy resistance.

### You are not what you create

When it comes to feedback, remember that any critique is about the thing, not about you. Your worth isn't defined by what you make.

### Barriers to creativity

- **Comparison.** Constantly measuring your process and output against someone else's.
- **Fear of failure.** Being afraid to take risks or make something imperfect.
- **Lack of inspiration.** Not being able to find the spark.
- **Perfectionism.** Chasing flawless results until you can't take the first step.

### Getting past them

Embrace imperfection, since creativity is a journey and not a destination. Stay curious. Give your ideas away and get other people's eyes on them. Be easy on yourself, celebrate small wins, and don't critique an idea until it's fully fleshed out. Trust the process.
