**Embers** — Captivating intro. What’s the scripture here? Reminds me of the intro to Busta Rhymes’ *E.L.E.*

**Smoke** — What’s that female sample? The juxtaposition of the tender melody over the intensity of the instrumental is brave. Especially for the first track. He carries the verse for awhile, but the vocal chops aren’t there so the performance is flat. This lower spot, “everybody’s drama gonna come out in some way,” is cool. I need more enunciation there though. Hard to pick up the lyrics.

**Traumatized** — These beats are interesting. They are hooking me and keeping me engaged. “Might mess around and get the whole place jumping.” The lead vocal needs to sit on top of the mix better. Might need better EQ. I’m missing most of these words. Have we fallen into mumble rap territory? This beat would work well on a video game soundtrack or something though.

**Numb** — These instrumentals are so huge but the mic presence is too laid back. It’s not working for my ears and what I’m used to. I want to believe and feel what the rapper is saying. I like that he’s experimenting with sounds and styles. I am not sure he’s the best at executing on his ideas though. Makes me think that Luke is better suited to be a producer.

**Water** — Another dope sample here at the top. I like the melodic choices he makes. Very repetitive song here. Catchy though. I dig how the instrumental adds and takes away layers, ebbing and flowing with the progression of the song.

**Burn Victim** — The first verse here is really cool. The inarticulate vocals afterwards are strange though. Again, the sounds he’s coming up with here are dope. However the execution of it all could still be stepped up a notch. This might be the best/coolest song so far though.

**Start Again** — I’m not feeling the straight song singing here at the top. I am not sure these lyrics are strong enough to be presented this way either. After a promising track like “Burn Victim,” this track brings a huge dip in sound and energy.

**Control** — Some of these instrumentals sound like Konata Small type arena bangers. I’m still not feeling like the vocal performances match up to the musical intensity. I wish I were there for this studio session. The vibe for the phrase “I found my strength on my knees” could be even better than it is. I need some guts to be thrown up on this track though. I want to feel it. Again I’m not mad at the choices made creatively here, but a very new artist trying to pull each choice off at will is too big of a challenge.

**Poise** — I don’t think pure singing is in his bag. This song calls for a feature. Break up the sound overall and get a friend to sing on this. The vocal chops aren’t there to be able to carry a song like this. Ok now he’s rapping. I don’t know that the pace of these rhymes fits with the style of beat here. It’s almost calling for a double time rap there.

**Let It Burn** — I would have shed the melody on this one and went with a straight rap. The bars are there. The ad-libs behind verse two need tucked back a little. This is the best flow on the album. And that “let it burn” hook is cool. I would ditch the pre-chorus we heard earlier and only do this for the entire song. Yeah, these are the best raps on the project.

**Ashes** — “Let It Burn” felt like a final song. I would have ended the project there. The more hushed vocal here is the best part: “I keep my eyes on you.” But the really loud falsetto, “you make beauty from my ashes,” is too shrill for my taste.

To conclude, Luke needs to surround himself with people who can push him to not make the same decisions over and over again, and even people to take some of those duties away. The style of his vocal performances sounded to me more like a reference track than an actual final product. If he went and got coaching for a year and came back to this, it’s possible he could do better, but at that point I would still suggest finding features.

The production on all this is cool, but I did find it too loud in spots, where the mix was clipping and distorting at the top. The sequencing was good for the most part; a lot of the songs felt like they belonged with each other. This wasn’t an album focused on lyrics, and I’d categorize it as experimental.

Luke has a long way to go in finding a lane to stay in, and my suggestion would be using his voice and creative thoughts in collaboration with others. He seems to have a lot of potential when it comes to creating mood and feel. The execution of it all is where the collaboration can come in. This was a big undertaking for a first album, and to me it’s way below the bar.
