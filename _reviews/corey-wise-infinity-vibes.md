### First Glance

There’s something going on in Maryland. This is my second review in as many weeks for an artist from this state. This is also the second instance of being my first time listening to the artist I review. This week I pushed play on *Infinity Vibes* by **Corey Wise**. Upon first glance, the artwork is well done and appealing with a futuristic/galaxy motif and** Corey **looking over his shoulder on the front. Artwork is a great way to garner listeners, so I was already prepped for something fresh.

The first track, “Take It Easy” begins pretty laid back but moves into some heavy bass. The instrumental lays a perfect bed for** Corey **to do his thing. Dripping with swag, he flows with ease, and boasts in Christ along the way. An impressive first track,** Corey**rolls with a lot of wordplay and heaps of similies. Initially it’s the Christ-centeredness of it all that jumps out to me, and is much appreciated.

“I don’t want your xans I don’t need em/ I just want to show you death has been defeated”

### Blazing Up The Beat

Track two is a track that I saw on some end of the year lists, which is what put me onto this artist. “Blessings Be” is a remarkably catchy tune, and it’s firing on all cylinders. There’s a pop influence to it with some auto-tuned singing and an upbeat tempo.** Corey**’s rapping here is melodic and again effortless. He’s a master on the mic. Lawren is the feature on this track and he blazes it. This track is a banger as well and I’m all in on these vibes** Young Wise**is creating.

Next, we hear a mother figure speaking over an intro before we get into the dark and haunting beat on “Badman.” I love the direction here, with this intro giving us breath before we change subjects. The decisions made in the production on this track are terrific as well, with pauses and the bass dropping in just the right places so as to highlight the performance.** Corey**has a laid back approach to his flow when some rappers can get overly aggressive, especially on this beat. He’s a natural, and there are so many quotable lines on this project I find myself stuck trying to catch ‘em all. (Push it to my ‘migos no Quavo.) One thing lacking in this song for me was that I wasn’t feeling the concept. The crazy thing is that it didn’t really matter. With production, performance, and direction all above the bar the concept didn’t derail the song for me. Who doesn’t like a redemption story anyway?

“Free Hope” is another track with a strong message that points to Christ. A stable performance with catchy singing. The music bed here consists of some chilled out Rhodes, and the hook is very repetitive. It’s good to have songs that stick with you and give you something to remember. However, this is probably the one song on the project I wasn’t feeling as much. There are a lot of appealing things about this song, but when it came to the content in the verses I just felt like some of the similies weren’t hitting. “Not chasing cheese like a Cheeto/laid down my life like a Frito” was pushing it for me. I appreciate artists who don’t take themselves too seriously, but I find these verses to be under the bar.

### Strong Production

Moving along we’re back into a big beat reminding me of some southern gangsta stuff I used to bump back in the day. “Die to Self” is another well produced track with intricate layers, switching from full to half time, moving in and out of drums beats and synths at certain points. “No sin was ever too big/…somehow I died to live.” This Chorus is terrific, and the rest of the pop influenced performance is at the bar.

Track 6 – This is my favorite track of the project. “Was Good” is fantastic, beginning with a music box-like sample and moving into some flapping bass. (Buckle up!)** Corey**starts out with some storytelling, and ultimately points to hope. This track also has another solid feature. Sometimes features tend to overshadow what an artist is trying to do with a track and can enhance or detract, but** Corey**carries the song on his own.  This song is above the bar all the way. Parris Chariz was just a tremendous added bonus to an already dope track.

I’m almost through the album and I’m not weary. The order of tracks keeps the slump factor at a minimum. On “Infinity Revival”** Corey**stays consistent, no changes to style or message. This song has a refreshing construct, with preacher dubs instead of hooks, and a Kanye West type beat with a soothing female jazz vocal sample. Still coming with his gift of writing similies – “kick it like soccer/litty like Flocka,” Corey points out hypocrisy in the world, and his conclusion is that we all need a revival. While there is still a bonus track left, I feel like this was a strong ending to the project.

### Repeat Button Is Necessary

The bonus track, “Otto Rocket” hits you in the face with nostalgia, a sample from the Nickelodeon show “Rocket Power.” Love the waves on this track, there’s even a Shiwan feature that belongs on a song like this. Track 8 leaves us with something fun, and like the rest of this project, something to click “repeat” on.

This project was a blast to listen to. While** Corey Wise**has been around for awhile, he isn’t very well known in the CHH scene. I’d say this project the bugle horn for those who are sleeping.** Corey** has his gift on display with solid writing, spouting off metaphor and similies, and unashamedly displaying the Gospel.  Not only that, but the production, the flows, the direction, and the fluidity of the project are all on point. Do yourself a favor and catch some *Infinity Vibes*,today. **Above The Bar.**
