The first Bar Exam of this week is Shyne On Me’s free mixtape “Barmonies: The Long Fall.” The artist from Washington, D.C. released this project at the beginning of December 2017 revolving around the theme that we as humans can easily fall into temptation. The first look at the cover is a bit confusing, there’s some fur and sun shades over the eyes of a determined face. The colors are vibrant orange and yellow, and the font seems a bit dated. Not sure if I’m too interested just yet. Let’s dive in.

“Running To You” - The first thing I notice upon hitting play is how this intro is setting the whole thing up to be extremely musical. To begin, there’s a live capture of what sounds like an upright piano, and then a muffled soul sample as the rap starts. The beat morphs as the song moves on and some real nice keys are layered in at the end. The performance here is chilled out, and feels like you’re sitting in a living room. Shyne’s flow is a bit mumbly, and it’s hard to decipher every lyric. This track is a compelling start to the work, laying numerous melodies over each other for a six minute introduction.

“The Longest Fall” - I really dig the overall style Shyne On Me is going for. It’s mellow and easy going. This track put to good use some piano loops and boom bap. There’s a lot of background vocal layering that drops in at the end of each line. “Set me free of destruction/future’s getting bright but the memories keep me humble.” …”Wounds all in my back/but my soul still intact.”

With Kayla Starks adding some vocal lines throughout, this has the workings of a great song. I’m struggling with how the song is mixed though. There’s too much blending going on, and the main vocals aren’t out in front enough for my liking. Too many effects are being used on Shyne’s voice and it’s distracting from what he’s actually able to do without effects. All the choices that are made need to enhance what’s already there… these unnecessary effects diminish the listening experience.

The beats chosen for this project are great. They compliment Shyne and his talent on the mic. Not only does Shyne On Me have a unique flow on him, he’s got a singing ability that is above the bar as well. The display on this particular track is heavy with similes, entailing how everyone fails, storms will come but the sun will shine again. The problem again here with this song is that Shyne can hold his own, but the mixing is getting in his way. There’s some phasing issues throughout, like there were some “stereo widening” effects used, and it results in just a wall of sound without depth.

This “Skeletons” track starts off so nice. There’s some singing at the beginning that rolls into a pastor overdub, then the beat drops. It’s got a Halloween vibe to it and it’s nasty. This song is straight hood. It’s got a great underground street vibe to it, and for me is the best track on the album so far. Yet there’s still mix issues here, and I’m trying to fight through it. I feel like all of these music beds aren’t far enough underneath Shyne’s performance, so he ends up blended into everything.

“Praying Knees” - Shyne On Me brings these songs out with confidence. He presents honesty and shares a lot about his faith walk on these tracks. Once more, the beat here is terrific. I really dig how it switches up for the last verse, and Shyne rides that beat like a pro here. “He paid the price now my debts is all clean/I owe Him my life now, head bowed, I’m down on my knees.”

“Autumn” has a jazz trio vibe to it, and Shyne remains consistent in his abilities here. I’m still disappointed in these mixes though. Man, I recognize some of these beats, not sure if they’re heavily inspired by other beats, or if this is a true mixtape. Regardless, the musical performance on this track is off the chain. Someone tell me who did this beat!

The next two tracks “Choices,” and “Lustfool” have a similar vibe and at this point I’m beginning to lose energy. Kayla Starks has a great hook there in the former track and helps bring that one along. The best part in this section though is Shyne’s last verse on “Lustfool,” which was on fire.

Alright I can recognize Jidenna’s “Long Live The Chief” being used as the beat here on “Electric Current.” Shyne flips this and makes it entirely an R&B track. “As long as we stand together in love we can’t be defeated.” Then to wrap up this project, “My City Soul” brings the same mixing issues as most of the tracks before.

To close out my review, I see a lot of promise in Shyne On Me for the future. His style is unique and he’s incredibly talented. As far as this project goes, for one, I feel like some choices that were made as far as the fluidity of the tracks could have set him up better. He’s a singer/rapper, and it seems like he stacked all of his flows at the front of the album. This sets it all up wrong though because there were next to no flows on the back half. While Shyne On Me’s singing chops are great, how those melodic songs fit in with the raps should be considered in the future.

I had a hard time listening to this album and that was solely based on the mixing. There were so many sounds and frequencies not appealing to my ear at all. I really wanted to enjoy this work and what Shyne On Me was doing, but I couldn’t. I am not a pro at audio engineering at all. However, I do know that when you decide to listen to music it should sound pleasant. So whether it was too much reverb, delays or stereo widening effects… in the case of this project I feel like less would have been more. I have to give this album an overall rating of Below The Bar.
